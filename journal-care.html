Keeping gold bright between functions

What to do the night after a wedding, and the three things that dull gold fastest at home.

Gold does not tarnish the way silver does. When a chain or a haram looks dull, it is almost never the gold — it is a film of perfume, sweat, talcum and kitchen oil sitting on the surface. That film is easy to remove, and easier still to avoid.

The night after a function

Take the jewellery off before you sleep, not the next morning. Wipe each piece with a dry, soft cotton cloth — an old cotton saree border is better than tissue, which leaves lint in the grooves. Let the pieces sit out for a few minutes before you put them away, because a warm piece stored in a closed pouch traps moisture against the metal.

The three things that dull gold fastest

  • Sprays worn over jewellery. Perfume and deodorant leave a sticky residue that collects dust. Spray first, dress, then wear the jewellery.
  • Kitchen work. Steam, turmeric and oil all cling to gold. Turmeric in particular can leave a stubborn stain on the white-gold and rhodium-finished pieces.
  • Cleaning liquids. Bleach, toilet cleaner and even some floor cleaners attack the alloy metals mixed into gold. Remove bangles and rings before you start.

A safe clean you can do at home

For plain gold — chains, bangles, kada, plain studs — take a small bowl of lukewarm water with one drop of mild dishwashing liquid. Soak for ten minutes, brush gently along the grooves with a soft baby toothbrush, rinse in plain water, and pat dry with a cotton cloth. Do not use hot water, toothpaste, soda or a metal scrubber. Toothpaste is abrasive; it will take the shine off a polished surface over time.

If a piece has stones, pearls, kundan, enamel or an antique finish, do not put it in water. Bring it to us instead.

Stone settings are held by fine gold cups and adhesive. Water loosens the adhesive, and a brush lifts the cups. Antique and nakshi finishes are deliberately matte — an enthusiastic polish at home will make a temple piece look like new brass, and that finish cannot be put back easily. Pearls are the most delicate of all; a wipe with a barely damp cloth is the most they should ever see.

Storing pieces so they last

Keep each piece in its own soft pouch or a butter-paper fold. Chains kink and scratch when they lie together, and a heavier haram will mark a lighter one. If your locker or bureau tends to be damp during the monsoon, drop in a small silica sachet and change it every few months. Fasten every clasp before storing — an open clasp is how chains end up knotted.

When to bring it in

Once a year is enough for most families: we ultrasonically clean and re-polish the pieces you actually wear, check clasps and stone settings, and tell you if a chain has thinned at the wear point. It is a small job and it is far cheaper than replacing a broken haram after a function.
