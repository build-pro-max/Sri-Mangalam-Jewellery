What the hallmark on your gold actually says

The marks on the piece, how to check them yourself, and what purity has nothing to do with.

Every piece of gold jewellery sold in India today should carry a BIS hallmark. It is a small set of marks, usually near a clasp or on an inner surface, and it takes about thirty seconds to check. Knowing what it says makes you a much calmer buyer.

The marks you should find

  • The BIS standard mark. A small triangular logo of the Bureau of Indian Standards.
  • The purity grade. Written as both karat and fineness — 22K916 for standard jewellery gold, 18K750 for lighter and stone-set work.
  • The HUID. A six-character alphanumeric code, unique to that single piece.

Ask for a magnifying glass at the counter. Any shop should be willing to show you where the marks sit on the piece you are about to buy — we do it as a matter of course.

What 916 means

916 is not a brand or a grade name. It means 91.6 per cent of the metal is gold; the rest is copper, silver or zinc, added because pure gold is too soft to hold a clasp, a stone or a shape. That is also why 24K is not used for jewellery — a 24K bangle would bend in ordinary use. For pieces with many stones, 18K750 is often the better choice: harder metal grips the settings more securely.

Checking the HUID yourself

The HUID can be entered in the BIS Care app on any phone. It will show the purity the piece was tested at and the jeweller it was registered to. If the code on the piece does not match the code on your bill, stop and ask. Your bill should always carry the HUID, the net weight, the purity and the making charges separately.

Purity tells you about the metal. It tells you nothing about the price you are paying for the work.

Purity, wastage and making charges are three different things

Two 22K bangles of identical weight can be priced quite differently, and both can be honest. The gold value is the same; the difference is in the making — hand-worked nakshi, cut-work piercing and stone setting take far more karigar hours than a plain machine-drawn band. What you should insist on is that the three numbers are stated separately on the bill, so you can see exactly what you are paying for.

Old gold and hallmarks

Older jewellery often has no HUID, because the scheme is recent. That does not make it impure. Bring it in and we will test it in front of you, tell you its purity, and give you a value on that basis — no guesswork, and no obligation to sell.
