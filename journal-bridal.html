Planning a bridal set without regret

The order to buy in, the two pieces worth spending on, and where budgets usually slip.

Most families arrive with a total budget and a long list. By the third shop the list has grown and the budget has not. The families who end up happiest do one thing differently: they decide what the bride will actually be seen in, and buy that properly first.

Start with the photographs, not the list

Ask which functions are being photographed and how the bride will be dressed for each. A muhurtham saree with a high neck needs a short attigai and strong earrings; a reception with a lighter drape wants a long malai that hangs clear of the pleats. Two pieces do most of the visible work in the photographs — the neck piece worn closest to the face, and the earrings. That is where weight and quality show.

The order we suggest buying in

  • Attigai or short necklace, with matching earrings. Worn at every function, seen in every photograph. Buy this first, with the best making you can afford.
  • The long haram. The heaviest single purchase. Temple or nakshi work here holds its value and its looks for decades.
  • Bangles. Four to six as a set. Comfortable weight matters more than count — the bride wears these all day.
  • Everything else. Vanki, oddiyanam, jada pieces, nose pieces. Add these only if the budget is still comfortable; many are worn once and can be rented.

Where budgets usually slip

Three places, almost every time. First, buying many light pieces instead of one good one — six thin chains cost the same as one haram and none of them will be worn after the wedding. Second, very intricate designs where making charges climb steeply for weight you cannot see. Third, a second full set for the reception; a change of earrings and a lighter malai reads completely different in photographs at a fraction of the cost.

Weight you can see is worth paying for. Weight hidden inside a design is only worth paying for if you love the design.

Weight against wearability

A haram that is beautiful on a stand can be punishing after four hours in a hot hall. Ask to wear the piece for a few minutes with the clasp closed, and turn your head. If it drags at the nape or catches on a blouse hook, it will be taken off before the reception dinner. We would rather lose a hundred rupees of weight than have a piece sit unused in a locker.

Give made-to-order work its time

Custom temple work and stone setting take weeks, not days, and the last few weeks before a wedding are exactly when every workshop is full. Tell us the muhurtham date on your first visit and we will plan backwards from it, so nothing has to be rushed or substituted at the end.

Come once with everyone who has an opinion

Bring the bride, her mother, and whoever else will comment — together, once. Decisions made with the whole family present are rarely revisited. Decisions made alone are usually unpicked at home and cost another trip.
